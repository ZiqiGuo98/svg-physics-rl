Ball-wall collision: can a model draw the correct collision frame by frame, without writing code?

Task: the model directly outputs static SVG frames; the grader extracts the ball's coordinates by id, first checks hard constraints (wall penetration etc.), then compares soft accuracy (contact time, restitution ratio, trajectory).

2. Task and grading setup

Task: the ball starts on the left, moves right at constant horizontal speed, hits the wall on the right and rebounds with coefficient of restitution e. The prompt gives the initial speed v, wall position, ball radius, e, frame interval Δt and canvas scale; the model draws 16 frames. Each task randomizes v, e, wall position and ball radius, subject to: e ∈ [0.3, 0.95]; at least 4 frames before and after contact; the end point after the rebound stays inside the canvas; the contact time does not fall on a sampled frame (within ±0.1Δt).

Ground truth (computed on the grading side): before contact x = x₀ + v·t; at the instant the ball surface touches the wall the velocity becomes −e·v.

Coordinate convention: use the ball's center cx; the prompt gives the x of the wall's inner face, and contact occurs when the center is at distance r (radius) from that face. Wall position and ball radius are always taken from the task parameters: the grader does not read them from the SVG, it only checks that the SVG values agree with the parameters (±1px); a mismatch counts as a format failure. The ball's y must stay constant. Parsing only recognizes circle#ball; other elements are not graded.

How the three quantities are defined on discrete frames

  • Incoming speed: least-squares line fit over the frames before contact; take the slope.
  • Rebound speed: the same fit over the frames after contact; take the slope.
  • The model's contact time: the intersection of the two fitted lines with x = x_wall − r. Not "the frame closest to the wall".
  • Diagnostic quantity: separately record "position error of the first frame after rebound"; it best distinguishes an e=1 mirror reflection from a true rebound with e.

Reward: lexicographic order of hard constraints then soft accuracy

Bad format:                      reward = 0
Any hard constraint violated:    reward = 0.2 × soft        (at most 0.2)
All hard constraints satisfied:  reward = 0.3 + 0.7 × soft  (at least 0.3)

soft ∈ [0,1], computed from three terms: contact time, restitution ratio and trajectory error (geometric mean or minimum),
each with a tolerance band: full score if the error is within tolerance, decay only beyond it.

Hard constraints (violating any one enters the violation tier): wall penetration (in any frame the ball surface crosses the wall face by more than the tolerance); turning back before reaching the wall (maximum x smaller than the ground truth's maximum x on sampled frames by more than 0.5·v·Δt); no direction reversal after contact along its own trajectory; rebound speed greater than 1.05× the incoming speed; per-frame displacement over 1.5·v·Δt, y drift, leaving the canvas, static throughout.

Success (fixed before training): no hard-constraint violation, and all three terms (contact time, restitution ratio, trajectory) within tolerance. Because full score is given within tolerance, success is equivalent to reward exactly 1.0: the success rate is a thresholding of reward, not evidence independent of reward. Independent verification needs an additional manual or VLM spot check of the videos, or a second independently implemented grader.

Order of setting tolerances: first set the physical tolerances (e.g. 10% relative error on e, contact time ±0.25Δt), then derive the trajectory tolerance from them, so the three tolerances do not contradict each other.

6. Related work and discussion

Related work and positioning (partly unverified)

  • SimuScene (arXiv 2602.10840): the model writes code, a VLM watches the video to judge qualitative questions, GRPO training. This work differs in that the model outputs per-frame state directly rather than code, and the reward is given by a deterministic grader.
  • VisPhyWorld (2602.13294): main text checked. SVG and Manim are only mentioned qualitatively in Figure 1 and Section 3.4 as "script-based backends"; the paper does not state whether the output is code or direct SVG markup, and gives no quantitative SVG results; the main experiments evaluate only Three.js and P5.js. So it cannot be used to judge whether "drawing SVG directly" has been done before, and this work makes no novelty claim based on it.
  • RL with Verifiable Physics (2607.10474), multi-reward RL for SVG-LLMs (2603.16189), GAUGE (2608.05948): only the search abstracts were read, not the full text; need to verify whether they conflict with this work.
  • Positioning: a small-scale feasibility demo of RLVR with a deterministic grader in a no-code, direct-state-output setting. No claim of being "first".
  • In the one-dimensional scene SVG is just a wrapper around coordinates, equivalent to outputting a coordinate list (e.g. {"ball_x":[…]}). The value of SVG is that it can be rendered directly to video for manual or VLM cross-validation, and extends to multiple objects. The SVG vs coordinate-list comparison was not run (limitation, recorded as next step): same tasks, same grader, only the output format changes; compare success rate and format failure rate.

Discussion

  • What it can show: in the controlled ball-wall scene, how physically consistent the model's direct state output is; whether RL can reduce severe violations and raise the success rate, and whether the improvement transfers to held-out parameters.
  • What it cannot show: it cannot prove "physics understanding"; the prompt gives v, e, x₀, Δt and the answer is a closed-form formula; the scene is idealized (single ball, rigid wall, no noise) and does not generalize to real video.
  • Limitations: few task types; the split between hard constraints and soft accuracy, and the tolerance sizes, are set by hand; success is equivalent to reward=1.0 and is not independent evidence; held-out variants not done; SVG vs coordinate-list comparison not done.
  • Still to verify: actual results of the grader self-check (Tables 2 and 3); ablation comparing continuous vs binary reward; diagnosis when reward rises but success rate does not (see Figure 4).
